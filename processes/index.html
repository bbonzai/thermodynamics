Gas Laws

Notes:

  • The Laws of Thermodynamics are not arranged/named historically but logically:

  • In 1850-1851, Clausius and Lord Kelvin independently came up with a statement of laws of thermodynamics systems, and chose to enumerate them logially, not historically.

  • The "0th" law wasn't even stated until the 20th century, and when it was, was thought to be even more foundational that all the others.

Zeroth Law

The 0th law defined temperature, and scientists realized it was so foundational that it logically preceded the other laws.

First Law (Energy)

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. The concept of energy is considered so foundational that logically, this law preceded the others.

\[ \Delta U = Q - W \]

where:

  • \(U\) = internal energy of a system
  • \(Q\) = heat transferred into the system
  • \(W\) = work done by the system

Second Law (Temperature and Pressure)

Defines the direction of energy flow and entropy (disorder).

Boyle's Law (1662)

  • If \(T\) is held constant, then \(V \propto 1/T\)

Gay-Lussac's Law (1802)

  • If \(V\) is held constant, then \(P \propto T\)

Charles' Law (1802)

  • If \(P\) is held constant, then \(V \propto T\)

Hot air ballooning was just introduced in the 1790's, and became popular throughout Europe. Charles was interested in laying a physical foundation for this phenomenon.

Avogadro's number (1811)

Avogadro found that: $V \propto $ Number of gas molecules.

Sadi Carnot's book (1824)

Carnot combined Boyle's Law and Charles' Law, but never articulated the combined results of both laws.

Ideal Gas Law (Benoit Clapeyron, 1834)

Clapeyron read Carnot's book (which had been neglected) and stated a law which Carnot used but never articulated.

An ideal gas is characterized by one where collisions between atoms and molecules can be ignored at room temperatures.

The ideal gas law [put history here] states that, for an ideal gas:

\[ P V = N k T \]

where:

  • $P = $ absolute pressure of a gas
  • $V = $ volume occupied by the gas
  • $N = $ number of particles in the gas
  • $k = $ Boltzman's constant
  • $T = $ absolute temperature of the gas

Third Law (Entropy)

Sometime between 1906 and 1912, Walther Nernst presented his "heat theorem".

\(S = 0\) when \(T = 0\)

The entropy of a perfect crystal approaches zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero.

Entropy (\(S\)) is a measure of disorder or randomness in a system. At absolute zero, molecular motion stops and a pure crystal reaches its most ordered state, meaning the entropy is 0.

You cannot reach absolute zero using any finite number of cooling steps.

Progress in quantum computing is directly tied to working aound the 3rd Law of Thermodynamics.
