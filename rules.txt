title: 'Rules for Thermodynamics competition, Science Olympiad 2027, Divs. B and C
---
Teams will:
* construct a device prior to the tournament designed to collect heat and 
* complete a written test on thermodynamcis concepts.

APPROXIMATE TIME: 50 Minutes 
TEAM OF UP TO: 2 
CALCULATOR: Class III
IMPOUND: Yes

## TEST PARAMETERS:

Written Test counts for 50% of final score.

Teams will bring a constructed device optimized to let a lamp heat water in a bowl
designed by the team.

### Rules for the written test:

There will be at least 3 questions from each of the following topics:
Answers must be in metric units with appropriate significant figures.

1. Thermodynamic ssytems
    a. Intensive and extensive properties of a system
    b. Definition of temperature
    c. The zeroth law of thermodynamics
    d. temperature scales and conversions
    e. definition of heat-related units

2. Phases of matter
    a. Phase transitions
    b. Phase diagrams
    c. Latent and Sensible heat
    d. Ideal gas law
 
3. Heat transfer
    a. Kinds of heat transfer
    b. Thermal conductivity
    c. Heat capacity
    d. Specific heat

4. Thermodynamic Processes
    a. Type of processes
       a1. Adiabatic
       a2. Isothermal
       a3. Isochoric
       a4. Isobaric 
    b. Cycles
    c. Engines 
    d. Efficiency
    e. First and second Law of Thermodynamics

5. History, particularly the contributions of:
    a. Lord Kelvin
    b. Joseph Black
    c. Joule
    d. Carnot
    e. Clausius
    f. Boltzman
    g. James Clerk Maxwell
    h. Max Planck  

### Rules for the Device Test

1. Teams will design and build a device to heat a cup of water using an overhead lamp.

2. Devices may not contain:
    a. Hazardous materials, such as 
        a1. asbestos
        a2. mineral wool
        a3. fiberglass 
    b. Commercially available components, including
        b1. Thermometers
        b2. Temperature probes
        b3. Coolers
        b4. Vacuum sealed devices

3. Within the device, participants must be able to insert and remove a 
9 oz disposable plastice party cup filled with room-temperature water. 

4. The device, including the cup of water, must fit within a cube, 35.0 cm on each side when
it is set up for testing.

5. Parts of the device may be be added to the water inside the cup

6. No part of the device may contact the water. 

7. Devices must not contain any energy source that would help warm the water, such as:
    a. electrical components
    b. battery powered heaters
    c. chemical component that will cause chemical reactions

8. Nothing may be added to the water.

9. The team will place the device on a surface, hereafter called "the testing surface",
like a table or countertop.
 
10.  Multiple identical setups may be used.

11. Each team will be provided a clamp lamp with metal reflector with a 100W incandescent, 
non-frosted bulb.  The lamp will be mounted facing down, above the testing surface, in such
a way that the device can sit centered under the lamp.

12. The bottommost point of the bulb above the testing surface will be 40cm.

13. Each team will receive 1 container of water containing 100mL of water from a "water bath"
containing room temperature water.  The water container will a be disposable, 
clear plastic 9oz. party cup approximately 4.0 inches tall.

14. The event judge will measure and record the temperature of the water as soon as it is
placed in the cup. 

15. The team will insert the cup of water into their device.  

16. At the start of the competition, each team will be told:
    * the height of the bulb above the testing surface
    * the heating time
    * the temperature of the room
    * the temperature of the water in the source water bath

17. After the device, with the water cup in it, is set up, the event judge will   
turn on the heating lamp for 1 minute.

18. The team must predict the final temperature after the 1 minute of heating.

19. At the end of 1 minute, the cup will be removed from the device.

20. The event supervisor will then measure the temperature of the water.

21. The team will be scored on 2 aspects of the competition:
    a. Their device achieved the hightest temperature gain of all the teams competing, for 30% of 
the final score, and
    b. Their prediction of the temperature of the water at the end of the heating period is the most accurate, for 20% of the final score.
